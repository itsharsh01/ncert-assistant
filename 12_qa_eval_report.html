Homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures can be differentiated based on their composition and distinctness of parts: * **Homogeneous Mixtures:** * These mixtures can have a variable composition. * An example of a homogeneous mixture is a copper sulphate solution. * **Heterogeneous Mixtures:** * These mixtures contain physically distinct parts. * They have non-uniform compositions. * Examples include mixtures of sodium chloride and iron filings, salt and sulphur, and oil and water.

Question 4

What is the formula to calculate the mass percentage of a solution?

The formula to calculate the mass percentage of a solution is: Mass percentage of solution = (Mass of solute / Mass of solution) × 100 This can also be expressed as: Mass by mass percentage of a solution = Mass of solute × 100 / Mass of solution

Question 5

Define the Tyndall effect.

The Tyndall effect is the scattering of a beam of visible light by the particles in a colloidal solution. It is named after the scientist who discovered this effect. For example: * A fine beam of light entering a room through a small hole shows the Tyndall effect due to the scattering of light by dust and smoke particles in the air. * Sunlight passing through the canopy of a dense forest shows the Tyndall effect, where mist (tiny water droplets acting as colloid particles) dispersed in air scatters the light. * A mixture of water and milk shows the Tyndall effect, while a solution of copper sulphate does not.
